\title{The speed of sound in a two-dimensional hard-disk gas:\\
a divider-mode measurement against the equation of state}

\author{Christoph~Haring \and Susanne~Still%

\maketitle


\begin{abstract}

% TODO-source: numbers below from 260919_A1v2_final_cs_vs_eta.csv (35 densities, 7873 trajectories)
We measure the adiabatic speed of sound of a hard-disk fluid from the eigenfrequency of a massive
divider separating two compartments, the method of Rom\'an et al. (2002), over 35 packing fractions
from $\eta = 0.0065$ to $0.76$ and nine divider masses per density. The measurement is compared
against the Kolafa--Rottner equation of state through the adiabatic mapping
$c_s^2 = (\kT/m)[Z + \eta Z' + Z^2]$ with no fitted parameter. For $\eta \le 0.4$ the agreement is
$+1.0\,\%$ on average and never worse than $1.7\,\%$ at $N = 100$; extrapolating to infinite system
size along a ladder of $N = 100$--$2500$ brings it to $\lesssim 1\,\%$. We give the finite-size law,
the estimator and its sensitivity, and a divider linewidth that recovers the Enskog transport
coefficient of the same gas.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
hard-disk fluid, speed of sound, event-driven molecular dynamics, equation of state, finite-size scaling
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Model and geometry}
Hard disks of diameter $\sigma = 2r$ and mass $m$ in a box $2L_0 \times H$, split by a divider of
mass $M$ and thickness $t$ at the centre; $N_s$ disks each side; $\kT = m = \sigma = 1$;
event-driven dynamics with the scientific backend (\texttt{--edmd-acc=0}).
% TODO-source: geometry constants from hspist3/validation/tests_20260913.py (N_TOTAL, N_SIDE, RDISK, H, WALL_T)
The divider is held for an equilibration period and then released; its position is recorded.

\paragraph{Effective length.} The centres of the disks in one compartment span $r$ to
$L_0 - t/2 - r$, so
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{eff}} = L_0 - 2r - t/2 , \qquad t = 0.05\,\sigma .
\label{eq:leff}
\end{equation}
% TODO-source: t = 0.05 proved by reproducing a stored trajectory, appendix A1 of 260919 report
Carrying $t$ lowers every $c_s$ by $(t/2)/(L_0 - 2r)$: $0.07\,\%$ at $\eta = 0.10$ and $0.50\,\%$ at
$\eta = 0.65$.

\section{The estimator}
\begin{equation}
P(f) = \Big|\sum_j (x_j - \bar x)\,e^{-2\pi i f t_j}\Big|^2 , \qquad
\nu = \arg\max_{f \ge \nu_{\mathrm{pred}}/X} P(f), \qquad X = 2.5 ,
\end{equation}
per trajectory, then the mean over seeds, then $c_s$ as the through-origin slope of $\bar\nu_M$
against $x_M = K(\alpha)/(2\pi L_{\mathrm{eff}})$ with $\cot K = \alpha K$, $\alpha = M/2N_s m$.
% TODO-source: estimator and sensitivity table in 260913_method_roman_fft_COWORK.md section 1.1
The one-sided edge exists because the divider carries a second, slow mode; the choice $X = 2.5$ is
flat over $X \in [2.08, 6.25]$ at 200 periods.

\paragraph{Why the largest bin and not a resonance fit.} Both put the nine masses on one line to
$0.27\,\%$, but the residual of the fit \emph{leans} with mass, $-0.286 \pm 0.020\,\%$ per e-fold in
$\alpha$ ($14.4\sigma$), while the largest bin is flat, $-0.030 \pm 0.029\,\%$ ($1.0\sigma$).
% TODO-source: 260913_method_roman_fft_COWORK.md section 3.1
The fit is the more precise estimator and the more biased one.

\section{Finite size}\label{sec:finitesize}
% TODO-source: 260917_A2_cs_vs_N_extrapolation.csv
Along $N = 100, 400, 900, 1600$ (and 2500 where available) at fixed $\eta$, $c_s(N)$ is fitted with
$c_\infty + b/\sqrt N$; the two other forms ($b/N$ and both together) bracket the systematic.
At $\eta = 0.02$, $c_\infty = 1.4614 \pm 0.0102$ against $1.4725$ from the EOS ($-1.1\sigma$); at
$\eta = 0.05$, $1.5699 \pm 0.0029$ against $1.5666$ ($+1.1\sigma$).

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{260919_cs_vs_eta_N100_vs_A2.pdf}
\caption{$N = 100$ against $N = 900$ and $1600$.}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What the offset is not.} The wall pressure of the same finite box is $7$--$20\,\%$ above
the bulk EOS and rising with $\eta$, which would imply a $+5$ to $+16\,\%$ excess in $c_s$ — five
times what is observed.
% TODO-source: appendix C2 of 260919_paper2_ramp_linewidth_fast_REPORT.md, 120 hold runs
The wall value is a surface contact value; the mode samples the bulk. The finite-size law above is
the measurement, not the wall pressure.

\section{The divider linewidth gives a transport coefficient}
% TODO-source: 260918_A1v2_linewidth.json and appendix A4 of the 260919 report
Linearising the piston equation of Malek Mansour et al.\ gives a damped oscillator with
$Q = 1/\mu$, $\mu = \Gamma L_y \sqrt{2/(\hat M N)}$, $\hat M = M + mN/3$. The measured FWHM of the
seed-averaged spectrum, with the bin width removed in quadrature, gives
$\Gamma = 0.314 \pm 0.038$ at $\eta = 0.112$, against the Enskog value $0.331$ --- consistent within
its $12\,\%$ error. The $M = 50$ row ($\alpha = 0.5$) fails the $K \ll 1$ assumption and is excluded;
the $M = 500$ row is $0.59$ of prediction and is not explained.


\section{Comparison with Rom\'an et al. (2002)}\label{sec:roman}
% TODO-source: validation/roman2002_remapped_20260922.py; his Table I, p. 850;
% figure 260922_roman2002_remapped_vs_KR
Rom\'an et al.\ measured the same observable in the same box ($N_s = 50$ per side, $H = 10$) and
report $c_s$ lying above spt and Henderson by an amount that \emph{grows} with density, from
$+1.3\,\%$ at $\eta = 0.112$ to $+12.8\,\%$ at $0.524$. That growth is the mapping, not the data.
They convert $Z$ to $c_s$ with a constant $\gamma = 2$; the correct adiabatic relation for a
two-dimensional monatomic hard-disk fluid is $c_s^2 = (\kT/m)[Z + \eta Z' + Z^2]$.

Re-mapping their own numbers through it, against Kolafa--Rottner:

Their density-dependent excess becomes a \emph{flat} $+1.10\,\%$ (spread $+0.91$ to $+1.46\,\%$),
and our $N = 100$ curve sits $+1.04\,\%$ above the same equation of state averaged over
$\eta \le 0.4$. Two independent implementations, twenty-four years apart, in the same box, with the
same offset: that makes the $+1\,\%$ a property of the $N = 100$ divider apparatus rather than of
either code. Their own size series (Table II, $N = 64 \to 4096$, $c_s = 3.81 \to 3.75$ extrapolating
to $3.74$) falls in the same direction as ours (\S\ref{sec:finitesize}).

As a transcription check, their spt and Henderson columns are reproduced to every printed digit by
the equation-of-state code used here together with their $\gamma = 2$ mapping.

The one point where the two measurements differ is $\eta \approx 0.52$: ours sits $+2.6\,\%$ and
theirs $+1.0 \pm 1.5\,\%$, which is $1.1\sigma$ on their error bar alone, with 100 trajectories
against our 25.
