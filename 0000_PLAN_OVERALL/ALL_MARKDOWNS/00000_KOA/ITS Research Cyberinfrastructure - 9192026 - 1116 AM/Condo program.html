The Koa Condo Program:
Owning a Piece of the Cluster

The Koa Condo Program allows University of Hawaii (UH) faculty and staff to purchase their own server ("condo") and add it directly to the Koa cluster. This gives your lab dedicated, priority access to computational resources that you own.

Think of it like owning a condominium in a larger building. You own your private unit, but you benefit from the building's shared infrastructure (power, cooling, security) and maintenance services provided by ITS.

Key Benefits of Ownership

  • Priority Access: Your lab's jobs will always have priority on the server you purchase.

  • Longer Runtimes: Run jobs for up to 30 days on your condo, compared to the 3 or 7-day limit on general community nodes.

  • Fully Managed by ITS:

    • ITS staff handle all system administration, including OS updates and patching.

    • All repairs are managed by ITS while the server is under warranty.

    • Standard warranties are 5 years for servers/CPUs and 3 years for GPUs.

  • Cost-Effective: You purchase the server (a capital expense) but avoid the high costs of building and managing your own separate infrastructure like networking, power, and cooling.

  • Community Contribution: When your condo is idle, its computing power is automatically made available to the general Koa community, ensuring the resource is always being used efficiently.

What Happens After the Warranty Ends?

Once the initial hardware warranty expires, you have three options for your server:

  1. Pay an "HOA" Fee: Keep your condo in Koa with priority access by paying a yearly fee (based on ITS co-location rates) to cover ongoing maintenance and infrastructure costs.

  2. Remain without Priority: Keep the condo in Koa without paying the fee. You will lose priority access, but you can still submit 30-day jobs to your node when it's available.

  3. Remove the Server: Have the physical server removed from Koa for you to self-manage or dispose of.

Important Note: ITS may remove an out-of-warranty server to make space for newer hardware
