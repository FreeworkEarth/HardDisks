Preemptable Partitions:
High-Risk, High-Reward Computing

"While the cat's away, the mice will play."

Preemptable partitions allow you to run jobs on idle servers owned by specific research groups ("condos"). This gives you opportunistic access to a huge pool of powerful resources that would otherwise be unused.

The catch? Your job can be preempted (stopped and requeued) at any moment if the owner of that server needs their resources back.

Understanding Preemption

  • What happens: If an owner submits a job, your job is stopped, and its progress is lost. It is then automatically put back in the queue to wait for another idle resource.

  • Best For: Jobs that can handle interruptions. This is ideal for applications that automatically save their progress (checkpointing) and can resume from where they left off.

  • Not For: Urgent, time-sensitive, or long-running jobs that cannot be easily restarted.

Reasons Your Job Might Stop

  1. Requeued (Preempted): The owner of the hardware needs their resources back. Your job is stopped and put back in the waiting queue.

  2. Canceled (Error): Your job will be permanently canceled (not requeued) if it exceeds its requested time or memory, or fails to use all requested GPUs.

At-a-Glance Comparison

Partition Name Best For (Use Case) How Resources are Allocated
kill-shared Single-node, fault-tolerant jobs (can be CPU or GPU). By the CPU core (share a node with others).
kill-exclusive Multi-node, fault-tolerant MPI jobs. By the whole node (you get the entire server).

Detailed Partition Information

kill-shared

This partition allows your job to request a specific number of CPU cores and memory on a single condo server, which you may share with other preemptable jobs.

  • Best For:

    • Lower-priority, single-node jobs that can be checkpointed.

    • Opportunistically using idle CPU or GPU resources.

kill-exclusive

This partition allocates entire idle condo nodes exclusively to your job.

  • Best For:

    • Lower-priority, multi-node MPI jobs that can be checkpointed.

    • Large-scale calculations that are not time-sensitive.
